Fault-tolerant services in Rust.

Write your service as a deterministic state machine. Turbofan's leader orders client input into a log, a quorum makes each entry durable, and every node applies it to the same service. If the leader fails, another takes over with the same state, and clients carry on without losing or repeating a message.

Pre-1.0, not used in production yet. Turbofan is simulated, fuzzed and tested over a real network and disk, but its APIs will change before 1.0. It is provided as is, without warranty of any kind.

What it's for

Systems whose state fits in memory, where every change must happen once, in one order every machine agrees on, and quickly. The guide's first chapter goes into each, and when Turbofan isn't the right tool.

Replacing a consensus stack

Not application servers plus a coordination service for leases, a log to order inputs, a database and deduplication tables: the service is the replicated part, so there's no lease to check, no offset to commit beside a write, and no message applied twice.

Sequencers and matching engines

The log is a sequencer: every input from every gateway in one total order, stamped with the leader's time, applied the same on every node. Price-time priority holds through a failover, with no resting order or fill lost.

Order management and risk

Orders, venue acks and fills as messages, every execution report sent once, in order. A limit is checked and taken up in one step, in the same state machine as the order. A day replays offline, exactly, to debug or audit it.

Ledgers, reservations, quotas

A balance checked and debited at once, the last seat or ticket won by one request, a rate limit counted in one place, a job or timer fired once though the node that set it fails.

Built so a failure loses nothing

Safe, thoroughly tested, then fast: a replicated service is only as good as its worst failover.

Exactly once, across failover

A client keeps one session through a change of leader: its messages are applied once and in order, and its responses arrive once and in order.

Deterministically simulated

A whole cluster and its clients run in one thread from a seed, through crashes mid-write, partitions, and lost, reordered and duplicated messages, with its guarantees checked after every event.

Sans-IO core

Raft and the node take inputs and return outputs: no IO and no clock reads. The tokio driver and the simulator carry them out the same way.

Fast

The leader sends to followers while it syncs its own disk, and what arrives during a write is written next, together: group commit without waiting.

Operable

Snapshots, members added and removed while it runs, learners, a standby that takes over a lost cluster, rolling restarts and upgrades, metrics, tokens and TLS.

Opaque to your service

The framework never looks inside your messages: they are bytes. Encode them however you like, or with turbofan-sbe, as the cluster does its own.

A service

Your code is a Service: every node applies the same log to it, so it must be deterministic, taking time from Ctx::now rather than a clock. This one answers each message with how many its session has sent; run gives it a command line. The guide's first service builds it up.

Performance

Three nodes on machines of their own, AWS {{bench.machine}} with 8 vCPUs each, and the clients on a fourth, over the network, 64-byte messages, from the repository's benchmark: the median of three runs, each batch synced to the instances' NVMe before it's acknowledged, as by default. The guide's performance chapter has every run, on other machines too, and how to measure your own.
